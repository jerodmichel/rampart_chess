Learn to Play RampartChess

RampartChess is chess with a deck of cards. You still win by checkmating the enemy king - but along the way you'll slip raiders past the rampart, break into your opponent's houses, and cast cards that add up to 21 to bring back fallen pieces or strike down enemy raiders. Each lesson below takes about a minute.

1. The goal

Just like chess: checkmate your opponent's king. White moves first. On each turn you make one move - either a normal piece move, or a cast move using cards.

GIF: goal-checkmate A short, dramatic checkmate from a late-game position.

2. The board

The board is 10 columns by 6 rows (rows a-f, columns 1-10). The bottom half is diamonds - White's territory; the top half is hearts - Black's. The gap across the middle is the rampart.

Some squares carry a card (card squares) and some are blank. The cards never move - they're part of the board. The back rows (a and f) are only playable on their three face cards: the Jack, Queen and King - the houses.

GIF: board-tour Highlight in turn: diamond half, heart half, the rampart gap, a few card squares, then the six house squares.

3. Your army, deck & graveyard

Each side starts with 4 raiders, a rook, a knight, a bishop and a king on the board. White starts on the diamond side, Black on the heart side.

You also hold a deck - all the Spades for White, all the Clubs for Black - and a graveyard holding 4 more raiders and your queen. Pieces in the graveyard can come back into play later; captured raiders and queens go there too.

GIF: setup Starting position; highlight each side's pieces, then each player's deck and graveyard.

4. Raiders & the rampart

Raiders are RampartChess's pawns. A raider moves one square in any direction and captures diagonally.

The catch: once a raider crosses the rampart into enemy territory, it can never come back.

GIF: raiders-move Select a raider, show its 8 target squares, move it; then a diagonal capture.
GIF: raiders-rampart A raider crosses the rampart; on its next selection the squares back across are no longer offered.

5. The other pieces

If you know chess, you already know these. The knight, bishop, rook, queen and king move and capture exactly as in chess. No square can hold two pieces, and you capture by moving onto an enemy piece. You can never make a move that leaves your own king in check.

GIF: pieces-move Quick montage: knight L-move, bishop diagonal, rook slide, king step, each with its target squares shown.

6. Houses

Each side has three houses: a Jack's, a Queen's and a King's. Only raiders can enter a house, and only the enemy's houses. A raider that enters a house stays there for the rest of the game - it can't move, and it can't be captured.

Houses are opened in order, and each one gives you a new power:

  • Jack's House - you can now cast cards to raise raiders and to strike.
  • Queen's House - your queen enters the game.
  • King's House - the enemy king is confined to card squares.
GIF: houses-jack A raider steps into the enemy Jack's House; show that it's now locked in place, and that the cast options unlock.

7. Casting: making 21

A cast move always uses exactly three cards that add up to 21. Some come from the card squares your raiders are standing on; the rest come from your deck, and you pick which deck cards to use - there's no random draw.

Card values work like blackjack: number cards are face value, face cards are 10, and an ace is 1 or 11, your choice. Deck cards you cast are used up. The card squares stay on the board for next time.

GIF: casting-21 Raiders on the 7 and 4 card squares light up, plus a 10 from the deck: 7 + 4 + 10 = 21. The deck card flies to the discard.

8. Raising a raider

Once you hold the enemy Jack's House, you can bring raiders back from your graveyard. Make 21 with either:

  • 1 raider on a card square + 2 cards from your deck, or
  • 2 raiders on card squares + 1 card from your deck.

(A raider sitting in a house doesn't count - it has to be on an ordinary card square.) Then place the new raider on any empty square in your own territory, except a house.

GIF: raising-raider Cast with one raider + two deck cards; a raider rises from the graveyard onto a chosen home square.

9. Striking

Holding the enemy Jack's House also lets you strike: capture an enemy raider without touching it. Make 21 with 2 of your raiders on card squares + 1 card from your deck, and pick an enemy raider to remove.

You can strike only raiders, and only in enemy territory - never in your own half, and never inside a house. (White strikes in rows d-e, Black in rows b-c.)

GIF: striking Two raiders + one deck card make 21; an enemy raider is struck from the board and goes to its graveyard.

10. The Queen's House & your queen

With the Jack's House taken, you can push a raider into the enemy Queen's House. On that same turn - for free, no cards needed - you place your queen on any empty square in your own territory (not a house).

You can only ever have one queen on the board. If she's captured, you can raise her back: 2 raiders on card squares + 1 deck card making 21, just like a strike.

GIF: queen-house Raider enters the Queen's House, then the queen is placed on a home square in the same turn.
GIF: queen-raise Later: a captured queen is raised back from the graveyard with a 2-raider + 1-card cast.

11. The King's House

After the Queen's House comes the King's House. Once you have a raider in it, the enemy king may stand only on card squares - every blank square is off-limits to it.

And if the enemy king is standing on a blank square at the moment you enter the King's House, that's an instant win: mate by capture. It's the one time in RampartChess that a king is actually taken.

GIF: king-house-restrict Raider enters the King's House; the enemy king's reachable squares shrink to card squares only.
GIF: mate-by-capture Enemy king on a blank square; raider steps into the King's House; the king is captured and the game ends.

12. Winning & draws

You win by checkmate (or mate by capture). The game is a draw (stalemate) if the player to move isn't in check but has no legal move. Either player can also claim a draw when the same position occurs three times (not necessarily in a row).

GIF: ending-stalemate A stalemate position: side to move is not in check and has no legal moves.
